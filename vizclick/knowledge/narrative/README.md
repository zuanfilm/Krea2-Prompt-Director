# Narrative

Narrative describes the underlying story, purpose, and meaning communicated by a Creative Representation.

Every successful creative work tells a story, regardless of its complexity.

A portrait, a product photograph, an architectural visualization, a cinematic frame, or an illustration all communicate ideas beyond their visible subjects. Narrative provides the invisible structure that gives those elements meaning.

Narrative is independent of medium, renderer, artistic style, and technical implementation.

It answers the question:

> **Why does this creative work exist?**

Unlike visual attributes such as lighting, composition, or color, Narrative is conceptual.

It defines the emotional and intellectual experience intended for the audience.

Narrative exists before the first light is placed, before the camera is positioned, and before any Actor performs.

It is the creative intention that guides every other decision.

---

## Purpose

The Narrative domain defines reusable concepts that describe the story being communicated by a Creative Representation.

Narrative serves as the highest-level creative context.

It influences every major creative discipline, including:

- Actors
- Performance
- Appearance
- Pose
- Dynamics
- Environment
- Lighting
- Camera
- Composition
- Color Palette

Every creative decision should reinforce the Narrative.

---

## Narrative as Creative Direction

Narrative is not a script.

It is not dialogue.

It is not a sequence of events.

Narrative represents the central idea the audience should perceive.

Examples include:

- Discovery
- Triumph
- Solitude
- Curiosity
- Luxury
- Transformation
- Conflict
- Celebration
- Hope
- Mystery

These concepts communicate intention rather than chronology.

---

## Narrative as a Global Context

Narrative belongs to the Creative Representation as a whole.

It is not owned by any individual Actor.

Multiple Actors may contribute to a single Narrative through different Performances and Relationships.

For example:

Narrative:

    Reconciliation

Actor A:

    Performance:
        Regret

Actor B:

    Performance:
        Forgiveness

Both Actors contribute differently to the same Narrative.

---

## Influence

Narrative provides creative direction to every other domain.

It answers questions such as:

- Why is the Actor present?
- Why was this lighting chosen?
- Why is the composition asymmetrical?
- Why is the camera positioned here?
- Why does the image feel calm or tense?

Without Narrative, creative decisions become isolated aesthetic choices.

Narrative transforms them into a coherent visual language.

---

## Relationship with Other Domains

Narrative influences but does not replace other domains.

Narrative does not specify:

- lighting
- lenses
- poses
- color palettes
- rendering techniques

Instead, those domains interpret the Narrative through their own concepts.

---

## Renderer Independence

Narrative never contains renderer-specific syntax, prompt engineering, or implementation details.

Its purpose is to preserve creative intent independently of technology.

Renderer Adapters determine how Narrative is expressed for each rendering system.
