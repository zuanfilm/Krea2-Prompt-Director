# Color Palette

> **The creative domain that describes the chromatic language of a Creative Representation.**

Color Palette is one of the core domains of the **Creative Knowledge Ecology (CKE)**.

Rather than describing renderer-specific color controls or image
processing techniques, Color Palette represents reusable creative
knowledge about how color shapes visual perception and emotional
communication.

Color Palette concepts are renderer-independent.

They describe **creative intent** rather than technical implementation.

---

# Purpose

The Color Palette domain provides reusable Creative Concepts that define
the chromatic identity of a Creative Representation.

Color Palette influences:

- Emotional tone
- Atmosphere
- Harmony
- Contrast
- Visual identity
- Symbolism
- Cultural perception
- Narrative emphasis
- Visual hierarchy
- Brand recognition

These concepts become building blocks for Creative Briefs and may be
combined with concepts from other domains through semantic reasoning.

---

# Design Principles

## Renderer Independent

Color Palette concepts never describe renderer syntax, software
parameters, or color grading controls.

Examples of excluded information include:

- RGB values
- HSV adjustments
- LUTs
- color grading settings
- saturation controls
- white balance

These belong exclusively to Renderer Adapters.

---

## Creative Representation

Color Palette concepts represent perceived chromatic identity rather than
technical color correction.

For example:

- Warm Palette
- Earth Tones
- Pastel Palette
- Jewel Tones

rather than:

- increase saturation
- reduce temperature
- shift hue
- apply LUT

---

## Reusable Knowledge

Color Palette concepts are reusable across:

- photography
- cinema
- animation
- illustration
- concept art
- game cinematics
- branding
- product visualization
- future rendering technologies

---

## Semantic Relationships

Color Palette concepts participate in semantic relationships throughout
the Creative Knowledge Ecology.

For example:

Warm Palette

may be associated with:

- Golden Hour
- Comfort
- Hospitality
- Autumn
- Nostalgia

Likewise,

Cool Palette

may be associated with:

- Winter
- Calm
- Technology
- Isolation
- Moonlight

These relationships enable probabilistic reasoning within the Visual
Meaning Layer (VML).

# Relationship with Lighting

Lighting determines how illumination interacts with a scene.

Color Palette determines the chromatic language perceived by the
audience.

Lighting answers:

> **How is the scene illuminated?**

Color Palette answers:

> **What emotional language do the colors communicate?**

Both domains work together while remaining independent.

---

# Relationship with Materials

Materials influence how surfaces interact with light.

Color Palette influences the perceived chromatic identity of those
surfaces.

For example:

Gold

is a Material.

Gold and Black

is a Color Palette.

---

# Relationship with Style

Style establishes the artistic language of a Creative Representation.

Color Palette establishes its chromatic language.

For example:

Style:

```
Film Noir
```

may naturally activate:

- Black & White
- High Contrast
- Desaturated Palette

Likewise,

Style:

```
Cyberpunk
```

may activate:

- Neon Palette
- Magenta
- Cyan
- High Saturation

These relationships emerge through semantic reasoning rather than fixed
rules.

---

# Role within VizClick

Color Palette concepts are selected by creators to establish the visual
identity of a Creative Representation.

The Visual Meaning Layer (VML) may activate related Color Palette
concepts through semantic reasoning.

Creators remain free to:

- accept recommendations
- ignore recommendations
- extend them through Custom Creative Intent (CCI)

The final Color Palette Brief represents both semantic understanding and
human creative direction.
